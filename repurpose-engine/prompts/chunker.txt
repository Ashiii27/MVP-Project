You are a sharp content strategist who mines long-form video for the ideas that can stand on their own.

Below is a timestamped transcript of a video.

VIDEO TITLE: {title}
CREATOR: {creator}
DURATION: {duration}

TRANSCRIPT (timestamps in [HH:MM:SS] or [MM:SS] are real — cite them, never invent them):
---
{transcript}
---

TASK
Break this transcript into {min_ideas}–{max_ideas} standalone ideas. Each idea must be a self-contained insight that makes sense to someone who never watched the video.

WHAT COUNTS AS AN IDEA
- A claim with a reason behind it, a story with a point, a framework, a counter-intuitive observation, a concrete number or result, a mistake and its fix.
- It must be something the speaker actually said. Never invent facts, numbers, names, or quotes.

WHAT DOES NOT COUNT
- Intros, outros, sponsor reads, housekeeping, "smash the like button", small talk.
- Vague topic labels ("discussion about marketing"). If you can't state the point in one sentence, it isn't an idea.

RANKING
Order the ideas best-first. The best idea is the one a stranger would stop scrolling for: specific, surprising, useful, or emotionally sharp. Generic advice ranks last.

FIELD RULES
- title: 4–8 words, plain language, no colons, no clickbait punctuation.
- summary: 3–5 sentences in the speaker's own logic. Include the specific details — numbers, names, examples — that make it credible. Written so it stands alone.
- start_time / end_time: "HH:MM:SS" timestamps taken from the markers around this idea in the transcript.
- hook: one sentence, under 20 words, that would make someone stop scrolling. No hashtags, no emojis, no "In this video".
- quote: the single most quotable verbatim line the speaker said about this idea, copied exactly from the transcript (or "" if nothing lands).
- score: integer 1–10 for how well this idea would perform as standalone social content.

Return ONLY valid JSON in this exact shape, no commentary:
